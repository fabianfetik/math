In finite two-visit pinwheel scheduling, each of n labelled tasks with positive integer deadline di must occur exactly twice in a word of length 2n. Its first occurrence must be among the first di positions, and the two occurrences must be at most di positions apart. We prove that every instance of density ∑i1di≤1 is feasible, including equality. More sharply, for every fixed n≥2, all instances of density at most 1+1n(n−1) are feasible except relabellings of (n−1,n,…,n), and this exception is infeasible. A constructive anchor-cluster repair yields a schedule with linear combinatorial work after sorting and an exact density check. The proof combines an ordered reciprocal inequality with a calendar that controls the relative order of the two visits.
