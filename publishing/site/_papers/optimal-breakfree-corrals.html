A breakfree corral of height h and order n has h rows on a cycle of length n(n+1)2. Each row starts at a chosen site and takes clockwise steps of lengths 1,…,n in some order. The hn arrival sites, one after each step, are pairwise distinct. Counting requires n≥2h−1. We construct a corral of order 2h−1 for every positive height. Together with the larger-order construction of Binięda, Dębski, Gutowski and Milewski, this establishes existence exactly when n≥2h−1.

At optimal order we consider physical partitions up to a common rotation or reflection, with the rows unlabelled. If Ah is the number of such classes with no nonidentity symmetry, then logAh=Θ(h2logh) as h→∞. For every h=2(mod4), h≥6, we also give an explicit family with an exact asymmetric orbit count and at least 2h24−h−1 classes. Every optimal corral of odd prime height is asymmetric.

We also prove a path-factor theorem for sufficiently large finite abelian groups of odd order. Given a near-spanning vertex set, a compatible set of nonzero differences and small initial and terminal sets, it produces paths covering the vertices and using each difference once. The construction determines the pairing between the prescribed endpoint sets. It gives a single cycle when the endpoint sets are empty. The single-path case supplies the terrace completion used in the diversity construction.
